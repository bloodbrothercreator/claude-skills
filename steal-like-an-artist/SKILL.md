# Steal Like an Artist

Virgil Abloh's "3% rule": change an original by 3% and it becomes yours. AI makes any visual possible, so the 3% is your taste. This is how we build it, one reference at a time.

The golden rule: **take the idea, never the execution.** We borrow the principle behind a piece, then make it in our brand's world. We never copy someone's picture, layout or style one-to-one.

## When this runs

Someone shares a reference (an image, a screenshot, a reel, a link) and wants a visual or video for a product, client or channel. Work through the steps with them in plain English. Don't skip the brief (Step 5): it's the whole method.

If there's no reference yet, start at Part 1 and help them find one.

---

## Part 1: Consume

### Step 1: Know where to steal from
Don't look in your own niche. Go sideways: know our field deeply, but look at others with a tourist's fresh eyes (Virgil's "tourist and purist").

- [Cosmos](https://cosmos.so/) for curated visual discovery
- [Are.na](https://are.na/) for channels built by creatives you respect
- [Savee](https://savee.it/) for mood and campaign boards
- [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/) for curated graphics
- [Colossal](https://www.thisiscolossal.com/) for contemporary art and craft
- Competitors' Instagram accounts: what's actually performing?
- Adjacent industries: fashion shoots product differently from skincare

**Save the thing that made you stop. That reaction is the brief.** At BBM, save references on a GlenDesk moodboard so the team can see them.

### Step 2: Consume on purpose
Saving isn't studying. Slow down on the thing that stopped you, and sit with why it works.

### Step 3: Study the artist, not just the image
Who made it? What were they doing across their whole body of work? The image is the output; the thinking is what we're after. If you can, look them up and note what they keep coming back to.

---

## Part 2: Create

### Step 4: Read the meaning
What is this piece actually saying? Read it before you remix it.

### Step 5: Pull out the idea, not the execution
Before touching AI, answer these with the user (look at the reference yourself if it's shared):

1. What is the single idea at the core of this image?
2. What is the unexpected element? (wrong scale, removed subject, UI in a physical space, product without the person…)
3. What does it make you feel, and why?
4. If you stripped the brand out entirely, does the concept still hold?

Write the answer in one or two plain sentences. **That's the creative brief.**
Example: *presence through absence. The product stays, the person disappears.*

If question 4 fails (the idea only works with the original brand), it's not an idea yet. Go back to the reference.

### Step 6: Brief the AI on the principle
Don't paste the image and ask for a copy. Describe the principle. Fill this in and use it (here, or in ChatGPT):

```
I'm creating a campaign visual for [product].

Creative principle: [the one-sentence brief from Step 5]

Emotional tone: [tense / warm / surreal / minimal / etc.]

Unexpected element: [the thing that makes it interesting]

Write me a detailed image generation prompt that applies this to my product.
```

When you write the image prompt yourself, make it specific: subject and product, composition and framing, camera and lens, lighting, colour, texture, setting, and what must NOT appear (the original brand, its logo, its exact layout). For lighting moods, the `relight-prompts` skill has ready-made ones. For YouTube thumbnails, hand over to `thumbnail-prompt`. For static ads built from a winning ad, use `static-ads`.

### Step 7: Generate the still
A strong still is the foundation for every video. Choose:
- **Nano Banana 2:** quick iteration, strong all-round realism and image editing.
- **GPT Image 2.0:** great with text in the image and higher quality for most uses, but more expensive.

Use them through Higgsfield, Claude + fal.ai, Figma Weave, or whichever platform the team uses. Attach reference images of **our product** and of **the composition we want**, never the original ad on its own.

### Step 8: Add motion
Only once the still is right. Choose:
- **Seedance 2.0:** multi-shot, realistic audio, strong realism.
- **Kling 3.0:** basic smooth motion on a still.

Write the motion prompt from the same brief: what moves, how fast, the camera move, and what stays still.

### Step 9: Add your 3%
This is the brand layer, and it's the whole point. Before anything is published, ask:
- Does this feel like it belongs to our brand (or the client's), or could it be anyone's?
- What's one thing we can add or remove to make the idea sharper?
- Is there a twist the original reference didn't have?

The reference got us 97% of the way. The last 3% is what makes it ours. If the answer to the first question is "anyone's", don't publish: change something.

---

## Part 3: Repeat

### Step 10: Run the loop until it compounds
One reference won't change anyone's taste; the loop will. Every cycle sharpens the eye. After each one, offer to save a short log (reference, artist, the one-sentence brief, what we changed for our 3%) next to the work or on the moodboard, so the team's taste builds up in one place.

**Consume. Create. Repeat.**

---

## What to hand back each time
1. The one-sentence creative brief (Step 5).
2. The filled-in AI brief (Step 6).
3. A detailed image prompt, and which model to use and why (Step 7).
4. A motion prompt, if it's going to video (Step 8).
5. The 3% check, with one concrete suggestion to make it more ours (Step 9).
